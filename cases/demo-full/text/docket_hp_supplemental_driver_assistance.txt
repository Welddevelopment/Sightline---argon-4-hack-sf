 1. OVERVIEW

 The 2015 Tesla Model S involved in this crash was equipped with a suite of driver
assistance systems (DAS). The basic function of a driver assistance system is to aid a driver in
performance of a driving task.

 The functionality of various DAS in a Tesla is afforded by the components that monitor
and collect information about the environment, analyze data and make determination about
possible hazards. Depending on the particular system, that information is used to either inform/
alert the driver or actively control the vehicle. This report discusses Tesla’s DAS in terms of its
(1) structural components, and (2) functional components. Furthermore, this report presents
information regarding the activation of various DAS during the crash trip, system updates and
discusses the process of forward hazard detection.

 2. TESLA’S DAS: STRUCTURAL COMPONENTS

 2015 Tesla Model S is equipped with several systems capable of monitoring and
analyzing the surrounding environment, each consisting of hardware components and
accompanying software analysis systems. These components are:

 • Radar system
 • Camera system
 • Ultrasonic sensors system
 • GPS mapping system

 The radar system uses super high frequency (SHF) 1 radio waves that broadcast and
receive waves using a single radar transceiver mounted behind the front grille of the vehicle.
Time of flight between the broadcast of a radio wave impulse and the reception of its return
reflected from a nearby object is used to compute distance. The radar is manufactured by Bosch;
the Bosch system conducts the initial data analysis, which is then used by Tesla and Mobileye
systems. Radar sensing is limited to a cone-shaped region forward of the vehicle with a specified
range of 160 meters (525 feet) under ideal conditions.

 The camera system employs a single camera mounted in the rear-view-mirror area of the
front windshield to receive light waves coming from a region ahead of the vehicle. The camera
system is manufactured by Mobileye. This optical data is routed to Mobileye’s visual processing
unit that performs pre-processing of the incoming optical data and provides reduced data to
various DAS components. The optical view is limited to a cone-shaped region directly ahead of
the vehicle. The maximum range of the camera system is limited by the resolution and the size of
the forward object.

 The ultrasonic sensors can emit and receive ultrasounds—an acoustic wave of high
frequency, short wavelength—and use the information to detect obstacles in its path. Time of

 3. TESLA’S DAS: STRUCTURAL COMPONENTS

 3.1. Overview

 The structural components afforded the functionality to DAS components. The DAS that
are present on 2015 Tesla Model S vehicles, including the accident vehicle, vary in the level of
assistance provided to driver. This assistance could be viewed on multiple scales. For example,

 • Some are continuous—use of adaptive cruise control, while others are discrete—
 occurrence of a warning from the lane departure warning system.

 • Some systems only alert a driver to a potential situation—a warning from the forward
 collision warning system, while others take momentary control of certain vehicle
 functions—automatic brake application by the automatic emergency braking system.

 • Some systems may be considered a convenience feature—adaptive cruise control,
 while others can be viewed as crash avoidance systems—lane departure warning
 system.

 The driver assistance systems in Model S Tesla rely on all or some structural components
that monitor the environment around the vehicle. Some of the safety and convenience features on
2015 Tesla Model S are standard, while others are optional.

 The following are some of the standard safety features on 2015 Tesla Model S: Speed
Assist, Lane Assist, Forward Collision Avoidance. 2015 Tesla Model S also offers an optional
Autopilot Technology Package. This suite consists of the following convenience features:
Autopark, Traffic-Aware Cruise Control, Autosteer, Auto Lane Change.

 3.2. Autopark

 This is a convenience feature that provides assistance when parking. It offers two
different functionalities that differ in the level of assistance.

3.2.1 Driver in vehicle

 W hat: Provides parallel and perpendicular parking on demand. The system controls
acceleration, braking and steering to move the vehicle into a parking space while driver monitors
the system’s automatic actions from within the vehicle.

 How: When traveling below a certain velocity (15 mph or 10 mph, depending on the type
of parking) a driver can request the system—through push of a button—to identify an available
parking spot. When the system indicates that it has identified an available location, a driver
pushes another button to request that the system initiate the parking sequence. If parking in
parallel, driver has to engage reverse gear before initiating the parking sequence.

 Structural components: This driver assistance feature relies on information from the
ultrasonic sensor system.

 A vailability: This is an on-demand system. It is available upon driver’s request, provided
it meets specific conditions. Parallel parking is available when traveling under 15 mph, and
perpendicular parking is available when traveling under 10 mph.

3.2.1 Driver in vehicle (Summon)

 W hat: While outside the vehicle, a driver can request the system to park and retrieve the

 How: Using the mobile app or the vehicle’s key, driver can request the system to initiate

Likewise, driver can also request the system to move the vehicle from the parking space to a

 Structural components: This driver assistance feature relies on information from the

feature from being used elsewhere.

 3.3. Speed Assist

 W hat: This is a safety feature that provides a warning when driver exceeds the speed
limit.

 How: The warning can be unimodal—visual, or bimodal—auditory and visual. The visual
warning (see figure 1) consists of a looming speed limit sign on the instrument panel—the speed
limit sign increases in size. The auditory warning consists of a chime. The warnings cease when
driver reduces the velocity to speed limit or after 10 seconds of the initial warning, even if driver
has not reduced the speed of the vehicle. The functionality of the system resets after the vehicle’s
speed decreases to below the warning threshold.

 Structural components: This driver assistance feature relies on information from the
camera system and the vehicle’s GPS mapping.

 3.4. Lane Assist

 Tesla’s Lane Assist system consists of multiple components, including (1) side collision
warning, (2) lane departure warning, and (3) automatic steering intervention. The first two
components provide only a warning, while the third component takes momentary and limited
control of steering—steers the vehicle away from the detected hazard—but it is not designed to
move the vehicle out of its travel lane.

3.4.1. Warning Only Components

 Side collision warning

 W hat: This is a crash avoidance system that provides a graded warning when another
vehicle enters Tesla’s blind spot or travels too close to the side of the Tesla. 2 The onset of a
warning is not dependent on Tesla’s action; side collision warning would occur even when the
Tesla has not activated lane change signal or drifted into neighboring lane.

 How: The visual warning is presented on the instrument panel (see figure 2)—a cone of
different colors emanating from a side/corner of the image of the Tesla; the location of the cone
corresponds to the location of the detected vehicle. The color of the cone indicates the proximity
of the detected vehicle—white, yellow, orange or red—with white representing a vehicle farthest
away. When the detected vehicle is closest, the cone turns red and an auditory warning—
chime—onsets.

 Structural components: This driver assistance feature relies on information from the
ultrasonic sensor system.

 A vailability: Active when vehicle is traveling between 18 mph and 85 mph. The system
can be disabled by driver. Default state is on, but driver’s selection persists until changed again
by driver (if driver disables the system, it will stay disabled until driver changes the selection).

 Restrictions: Dependent on the proper functioning of the ultrasonic sensors. Extreme
weather conditions—heavy snow and rain, fog, extremely hot or cold temperatures—as well as
interference by other electrical equipment using ultrasonic waves may degrade the performance
of this system.

 A ctivation in the accident vehicle: The recorder data that NTSB investigators received
from Tesla does not provide information about the activation of Side Collision Warning system.
NTSB investigators cannot determine whether the system (1) was available (i.e., no turned off)
or (2) activated during the trip on which the crash occurred.

3.4.2. Active Assistance

 Automatic Steering Intervention

 W hat: This is a crash avoidance system that provides automatic corrective steering
intervention when Tesla drifts into or close to an adjacent lane in which another vehicle is
detected.

 How: The automatic steering correction brings the Tesla back to the center of its own
traveling lane. Concurrent to the steering correction, a visual warning is presented on the
instrument panel.

 Structural components: This driver assistance feature relies on information from the
ultrasonic sensor and camera systems.

 A vailability: Active when vehicle is traveling between 45 mph and 85 mph. The default
state is on; driver cannot disable it. However, the system is not active when Autosteer (see
section 3.7) is engaged.

 Restrictions: It is dependent on the presence of lane markings and proper functioning of
the camera system, as well as the proper functioning of the ultrasonic sensors. The performance
is degraded when the following conditions are present: congestion (if driving to close to a
vehicle in front), bright lights, narrow or winding roads, heavy shadows on lane markers, and
poor visibility (due to snow, rain and fog).

 A ctivation in the accident vehicle: The system cannot be disabled by driver. However,
since Autosteer was active at the time of the crash, as well as for the majority of that trip,
Automatic Steering Intervention would not have been active.

 3.5. Forward Collision Avoidance System

 Tesla’s Forward Collision Avoidance System consists of (1) forward collision warning
(FCW), which only provides a warning regarding the forward hazard, and (2) automatic
emergency braking (AEB) which automatically applies brakes to prevent or mitigate a rear-end
collision.

3.5.1 Warning Only

 Forward Collision Warning

 W hat: This is a crash avoidance system that provides a visual and auditory warning
regarding a potential forward collision (rear-end crash).

 How: The visual warning consists of an icon of a red vehicle presented in front of the
image of the Tesla on the instrument panel (see figure 3). The auditory warning consists of a
chime. The warnings remain until driver brakes or steers away from the forward hazard.

 Timing and Testing: Driver can select the timing of the warning to occur earlier or later.
A later selection indicates a warning that occurs when a Tesla is closer to the lead vehicle, and an

 The timing of the warnings are based on distance between the subject vehicle and the
detected forward vehicle, and their velocities. Those dynamics are used to determine the time

onset is not fixed.

 In an effort to further research the latency of warnings, and determine representative
values for some crash scenarios, NTSB investigators examined NHTSA’s New Car Assessment
Program (NCAP).The NCAP consists of a 5-star safety ratings program that provides consumers
with information regarding passenger vehicle crashworthiness and rollover safety. 3 Driver
assistance technologies are not included in the 5-star safety rating. Certain DAS technologies like
forward collision warning receive recognition in the form of an icon indicating the presence of
that safety technology, located next to the vehicle’s star rating; however, this icon appears only
on the NCAP website.

 Examination of the NHTSA’s NCAP revealed that 2015 Tesla Model S received
recognition for the forward collision warning technology. In order to receive this recognition, the
tested vehicle has to meet NHTSA’s minimum performance standards—the warning has to occur
within a specific time period—as tested by NHTSA or self-certified by the manufacturer. The
investigators did not determine whether NHTSA tested this vehicle’s forward collision warning
system.

 NTSB investigators also examined European NCAP (EuroNCAP) and Australian NCAP
(ANCAP); however, those agencies did not test 2015 or 2016 Model S.

 Structural components: This driver assistance feature relies on information from the radar
and camera systems.

 A vailability: Available when the vehicle is traveling between 5 mph and 85 mph. FCW is
available on all road types. A driver can select one of the four options: (1) early warning, (2)
medium latency warning (3) late warning, (4) warning is off. The default state of the system is
medium warning, but driver’s selection persists until changed again by the driver (if a driver
turns the warning off, it will remain disabled until driver changes it again).

 Restrictions: The accuracy of this system can be affected by road geometry (winding
roads, sharp curves, or large slopes), weather conditions (rain, ice, fog), brightness (from
oncoming headlights or direct sunlight) or interference (electronic interference with radar waves
or visible obstruction such as mud on radar). For additional limitations of this system, including
detection of forward hazards, see section 3.9.

not contain any information indicating that FCW was activated at any point during the crash trip.

3.5.2. Active Assistance

 Automatic Emergency Braking

 W hat: This is a crash avoidance system that automatically applies brakes when the
system determines that a frontal collision is unavoidable. The system is designed to mitigate the
severity of the crash, although in some cases may even prevent the collision. When the system
automatically applies brakes, it also presents visual and auditory alerts.

 How: The braking is automatic. The visual warning consists of a rectangular bar
presented in the instrument panel that reads “Emergency Braking in Progress”. The auditory
warning consists of a chime.

 When AEB activates, the system applies up to full braking force. The applied force
depends on surface friction, but can be up to 1 G. The AEB remains engaged until (1) driver
takes a specific action, (2) the forward hazard is no longer detected, or (3) the velocity of the
Tesla is reduced by 25 mph or it has stopped (this can occur if AEB engages when traveling less
than 25 mph).

 Disengagement: The following specific actions by driver would disengage the AEB after
initial activation: (1) initiates an avoidance steering maneuver, (2) presses the accelerator pedal,
or (3) applies and then releases the brake. However, if driver applies brakes during automatic
braking, but does not fully release them, the AEB remains engaged until the end of its cycle. If a
driver applies brakes with less than full braking force, the AEB remains engaged at full braking
force until the end of its cycle.

 3.6. Traffic-Aware Cruise Control - Component of Autopilot

 W hat: Tesla’s Traffic-Aware Cruise Control (TACC) is a convenience feature; it is an
adaptive cruise control system that maintains the set cruise speed, applies brakes when
approaching a slower moving lead vehicle to preserve a following distance, or accelerates to the
set cruising speed when forward area is no longer obstructed.

 How: TACC detects vehicles or objects in path of the Tesla. After driver engages
TACC—selects a cruising speed—the system (1) maintains the set cruise speed as long as the
forward area is not obstructed, (2) decelerates the vehicle when it detects a vehicle ahead and
then maintains a set following distance, and (3) resumes the set cruise speed when the forward
area is no longer obstructed. When necessary to maintain a set following distance, TACC applies
braking force that results in deceleration of up to 0.5G.

 Driver can select the following distance—how closely to follow a vehicle in front. The
following distance selection ranges from 1 to 7, with the lower number indicating closer
following distance.

 Structural components: This driver assistance feature relies on the information from the
radar and camera systems.

Williston, FL – Human Performance Factors Factual Report: Supplemental Page 13 of 23

 Disengagement: TACC disengages if (1) driver deactivates TACC with the cruise control
lever, (2) driver presses the brake pedal, (3) velocity drops below 18 mph and the system has not
detected a vehicle in front, (4) driver’s seat belt unbuckles, or (5) the driver shifts to neutral.

 Interaction with FCW and AEB : When TACC is engaged and the system detects a
forward hazard, but determines that TACC braking would not be sufficient to prevent a rear-end
crash, the system brakes up to its limit (0.5 G), but also initiates a brake capacity warning
(BCW). The BCW alert is identical to the FCW alert; driver cannot distinguish between them.
The BCW activates even if FCW system is turned off.

 If a driver does not respond to the BCW alert, Tesla’s AEB engages, but only if it has not
been turned off. If AEB has been turned off at the start of the ignition cycle, TACC could still be
engaged and brake up to its limits, and BCW would still activate, but automatic emergency
braking would not occur.

 A vailability: This is an on-demand system. It is available upon driver’s request,
provided the vehicle is traveling above 18 mph. TACC can also be engaged even if Tesla is
stationary, if the system has detected a vehicle in front. The maximum allowed cruising speed is
90 mph, regardless of speed limit.

 Restrictions: The accuracy of this system can be affected by road geometry (winding
roads, sharp curves, or large slopes), weather conditions (rain, ice, fog), brightness (from
oncoming headlights or direct sunlight) or interference (electronic interference with radar waves
or visible obstruction such as mud on radar).

 The vehicle’s manual contains multiple warnings regarding the use of TACC (see figures
4 and 5). The warnings illustrate the limitations of the system—cannot detect all forward
hazards— instruct drivers not to rely on the system to avoid collisions, and caution drivers to
always attend to the road ahead. For additional limitations of this system, including detection of
forward hazards, see section 3.9.

 W hat: Tesla’s Autosteer is a convenience feature that automatically steers the vehicle to
keep it within its traveling lane. Autosteer can be engaged only after activating TAAC; Autosteer
cannot operate without TACC.

 When TACC and Autosteer are both engaged, the system (1) monitors the environment
on the traveling path, (2) maintains the set cruise velocity, (3) maintains vehicle’s position in the

 How: Autosteer detects lane markings and presence of other vehicles and objects ahead to
maintain the vehicle within the lane. When engaged, an icon depicting a blue steering wheel

 Autosteer disengages if any of the conditions that would disengage TACC occur (see

 Autosteer disengages if a driver starts steering manually and applies steering torque that
exceeds a fixed threshold; however, TACC remains engaged.

 Autosteer disengages—after a series of warnings—if the system does not detect driver-
applied steering wheel torque for a prolonged period (i.e., driver’s hands on the steering wheel).
Upon request by NTSB investigators, Tesla provided the logic detailing the conditions that affect
the timing of the onset of the initial warning, which is a visual alert. This timing depends on
several factors, including (1) the speed of a Tesla, (2) presence of a vehicle ahead of a Tesla, and
(3) lateral acceleration. Also, any interactions with vehicle controls (e.g., change in set cruise
speed) reset the hands-off counter.

 The initial warning occurs earlier when traveling at lower speed or when another vehicle
ahead of a Tesla is not detected, and later when traveling at higher speeds or another vehicle
ahead of a Tesla is detected (see figure 7). For example:

 3.8. Auto Lane Change – Component of Autopilot

 W hat: This is a convenience feature that automatically moves the vehicle into adjacent
lane upon demand. Auto Lane Change can be activated only when Autosteer is engaged.

 How: When Autosteer is engaged, a driver can request the system—by activating the turn
signal—to move the vehicle into an adjacent travel lane. The system monitors the Tesla’s blind
spot and forward to determine the availability of the target lane before executing the lane change.

 The system would execute driver’s lane change request only when (1) camera’s view is
unobstructed, (2) the system does not detect a vehicle in the target lane or in Tesla’s blind spot,
and (3) the system can detect the outside of the target lane after initiating the lane change. If the
system does not detect the outside of the target lane, it cancels the Auto Lane Change and
Autosteer. At that time, the system also presents a visual prompt on the instrument panel,
instructing the driver to immediately take over the steering wheel.

 Structural components: This driver assistance feature relies on information from the
camera and ultrasonic sensor systems.

 A vailability: This is an on-demand system. It is available upon driver’s request, provided
it meets specific conditions. The system can be activated only when Auto Lane Change has been
enabled, Autosteer is engaged and Tesla is traveling at least 30 mph. The default state of the
system is “on”, but driver’s selection persists until changed again by the driver (if a driver turns
the system off, it will remain unavailable until driver changes it again).

 3.9. Forward Hazard Detection

 Several functional components of Tesla’s DAS rely on system’s capability to detect
forward hazards and lane markings in order to provide warnings, initiate automatic braking or
automatically maintain the vehicle within a lane. The components that rely on detection of
forward hazards include: FCW, AEB, TACC and Autosteer. When it comes to the detection of
obstacles, the forward hazard detection systems are designed to detect slow, decelerating and
stopped vehicles moving in the same general direction as Tesla.

 Detection of forward hazards is typically made by interpreting input from both camera
and the radar systems. While both of these systems perform detection of forward objects, they
have specialized functions. Camera system performs object recognition and classification
processes, while the radar system is used to determine longitudinal measurements—how far
away the forward hazard is located, and to determine the relative velocity of the hazard.

 Potential forward hazards are classified based on (1) specific distance measurement by
the radar system and (2) detection and appropriate classification by the camera system. Most
classes of objects are required to meet both criteria. An exception is a specific class of forward
hazards that are classified as In-Path Stationary Objects (IPSO).

 An object is classified as an IPSO if it is associated with a stationary radar target—
specific distance measurement from the radar system—and the camera system does not detect a
recognized object at that distance, but does detect lack of free space or clear path in travel
direction of the Tesla. For example, a highway overpass would likely not be classified as IPSO,
even though a radar system may detect the overpass as a stationary object, but camera system
would detect available free space in the vehicle’s path underneath the overpass.

 Additionally, through the radar system, the Tesla can also detect a vehicle in front of the
vehicle directly in front of the Tesla. In such cases, radar system can obtain a reflection of the
wave underneath the vehicle directly in front of the Tesla; yet, the camera system would not be
able to detect the obscured vehicle.

 3.10. Software Updates

 At the time of the crash, the accident vehicle had the latest version of system software—
version 7.1—which controls and monitors all of the vehicle’s electronic components, including
DAS. The system software is updated wirelessly on Model S vehicles. When a new version of
system software becomes available, a prompt appears on the touchscreen informing the driver to
schedule the installation of the update. Software update can be performed only when the vehicle
is parked.

 At the beginning of the first ignition cycle after an update to system software, a prompt
appears on the touchscreen informing the driver of the changes that were made as a result of the
latest software update.

 Since the crash, Tesla issued several updates to the system. Version 8.0, which was
released on September 21, 2016, included changes that affected functioning of Autosteer
component. More specifically, the changes affected the conditions of disengagement. While in
the version 7.1—on which the accident vehicle operated—the number of hand-off warnings was
not limited, version 8.0 introduced such limits.

 Version 8.0 reduced the time that the system allows a driver not to place hands on the
steering wheel (see figure 10). According to Tesla, the maximum hands-off time was 5 minutes
in version 7.1. In version 8.0, the maximum allowed hands-off time was reduced to 3 minutes
when traveling at speed greater than 45 mph. When traveling between 8 and 45 mph, the
maximum hands-off time is 10 minutes. When traveling less than 8 mph, the system does not
provide warnings to indicate hands-off driving.
